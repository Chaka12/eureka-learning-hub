# Content Guide

A short guide to **what to write** in each section of the site. Most school sites fail
not because of bad design but because the copy is generic. Use this guide to write
copy that sounds like a real school with real kids.

---

## The core principle

**Specific beats generic.** "We are a leading learning centre" tells a parent nothing.
"Our Year 6 maths cohort jumped an average of 1.4 grades in one term" tells them a lot.

When in doubt, replace any adjective with a number, a name, or a story.

---

## Hero (top of page)

You have **3 seconds** before a visitor decides to scroll or leave. Three things matter:

1. **The brand** — logo + name
2. **The promise** — what you do, for whom
3. **The vibe** — tone, energy, professionalism

### Tagline

> *“Say It and It Becomes.”*

If that's your real motto — keep it, it's distinctive and memorable. If not, replace it
with a tagline that:

- Is **short** (under 8 words)
- Captures your **belief** about education
- Is **specific to you** (not something any school could say)

Examples:
- *"Big confidence for small humans."*
- *"Where curious kids become capable adults."*
- *"Maths, made click."*

### Subtitle (one sentence under the tagline)

**Formula:** `[Who you are] + [who you serve] + [what they get]`

Examples:
- *"Eureka Learning Hub is a community-rooted learning centre that helps primary and
  secondary students master maths, English, and science — in small classes with tutors
  who know their name."*
- *"A Saturday enrichment programme for ages 6–14, built around hands-on science,
  creative writing, and debate."*

### Call-to-action buttons

Two is the magic number:
1. A **primary** action (what you most want — usually "enrol", "book a tour", or
   "explore programs").
2. A **secondary** action (lower commitment — "contact us", "read FAQs").

## About

### Length

About 100–180 words across **two short paragraphs**. Anything longer and visitors
won't read it.

### Structure

- **Paragraph 1 — Origin.** Why does this school exist? What problem does it solve? Who
  started it and why?
- **Paragraph 2 — What you do today.** Be specific. "Tutoring for ages 6–16, plus
  holiday camps and parent workshops."

### Sidebar: Mission / Vision / Values

These are the classic trio. Write them like a parent would understand them — avoid
corporate jargon.

- **Mission** (what you do every day): "To give every student the tools, the
  confidence, and the words to build their own future."
- **Vision** (what success looks like long-term): "A generation of young people who
  speak possibility into reality."
- **Values** (3–5 short phrases, ideally in `Word: meaning` format):
  - *Belief — every child can.*
  - *Excellence — in teaching and character.*
  - *Community — we rise together.*

## Programs

### How many to list

**4–6** is the sweet spot. More than that, and the page looks like a phone book. Less
than 4, and parents wonder if you do anything.

### For each program, include

- **A clear name** that a parent would recognise ("Exam Prep", not "Academic Outcomes
  Optimisation Programme").
- **An emoji or icon** that hints at the program (📚 tutoring, 🎯 exam prep, 🧠 clubs).
- **One sentence on who it's for.**
- **One sentence on what they get.**
- **One sentence on the outcome** ("students typically improve by 1–2 grades").

### Bad → Good

❌ "Our tutoring program is designed to meet the diverse needs of learners across
multiple modalities."

✅ "One-on-one and small-group tutoring in maths, English, and science for primary
and secondary students — including exam prep, homework help, and catch-up support."

## Why us / differentiators

### Pick 4–6, not 12

You might have 15 things you're proud of. Pick the **6 that matter most to a parent
making a decision**:

- Personalised learning
- Small class sizes (with the number — "max 6 per group")
- Qualified tutors (with the credential — "all tutors are degree-qualified teachers")
- Track record (with a number — "98% of our Year 6 students improve by at least one grade")
- Safe / modern facilities
- Affordable / transparent pricing

### Format

For each, write **one short sentence**. Long paragraphs of marketing copy in this
section kill the page.

## Gallery

### What to photograph

In rough priority order:
1. **Students learning** (engaged, hands-on, smiling)
2. **Tutors teaching** (the warmth, not just the whiteboard)
3. **The space** (classrooms, common areas, signage)
4. **Events** (graduations, open days, holiday camps)
5. **Student work** (projects on walls, trophies, displays)

### Photo rules

- **Always get written photo consent** from parents (especially for under-18s).
- Aim for **landscape orientation**, ideally 4:3 or 3:2.
- Minimum **1200px wide** for crisp display.
- Compress before upload ([squoosh.app](https://squoosh.app) is great).
- **Always** write meaningful alt text describing the photo. This helps accessibility
  *and* Google Images SEO.

### Alt text formula

`"[Subject] doing [action] in [place], with [optional detail]"`

❌ "Image 1"
✅ "Three Year 4 students working together on a science experiment at Eureka Learning Hub."

## Testimonials

### How to get them

1. **Ask your happiest families** by email or WhatsApp. Make it easy — give them 2–3
   prompt questions ("What was the problem you came to us with? What changed?").
2. **Always get written permission** to publish. A short email is enough; keep it.
3. **Use their first name and a role** ("Amina K., Parent") — never full names of
   minors.

### How to write them up

Keep them under 60 words. Long quotes lose impact. If the original is longer, **edit
for clarity** with permission.

### If you don't have any yet

It's fine to launch with two or three placeholders. Replace with real ones as you
collect them. **Never fabricate testimonials** — it's unethical and, in many
jurisdictions, illegal.

## FAQ

### How to choose the questions

The best FAQ questions are the ones you've been asked **three times already**. Check
your WhatsApp / email / phone-call log.

### How to write the answers

- **Direct, short, friendly.**
- **End with a call to action** when appropriate ("Contact us to enrol" / "Book a
  free consultation").
- If the answer is genuinely long, **link out** to a fuller explanation rather than
  embedding a wall of text.

### Common questions to consider

- What ages do you teach?
- What does a typical week look like?
- How do you measure progress?
- What are your fees / do you offer scholarships?
- How do I enrol my child?
- Do you offer online or hybrid classes?
- What is your cancellation / refund policy?
- Are your tutors qualified?
- How do you handle children with learning differences?
- What safety measures are in place?

## Contact

### Be findable

Include every channel where you exist. A parent searching for you at 11pm with a
worried question should be able to find **at least one way** to reach you:

- Phone
- Email
- Facebook (yes, list it — for many families, Messenger is the primary contact method)
- Physical address (with hours)
- Optional: WhatsApp business number

### Form fields

Only ask for what you actually need. Each extra field reduces completion rate by
~10%. Stick to:

- Name
- Email
- Phone (optional)
- "What are you interested in?" (a `<select>` with your program names)
- Message

**Don't** ask for child's age, school year, etc. on first contact — get the conversation
started, then ask for the details in your reply.

## Tone of voice

### The Eureka voice

- **Warm** — parents are trusting you with their children. Be human.
- **Confident but not boastful** — let your track record speak; don't say "the best".
- **Specific** — always trade adjectives for numbers, names, stories.
- **Respectful of children** — never talk down to students; they're the customer too.
- **Community-rooted** — use "we", "our families", "our neighbourhood" rather than
  "the institution" or "the academy".

### Voice test

Read any paragraph aloud. Does it sound like something a real teacher would say to a
parent at pickup? If not, rewrite it.

---

## Things to never do

- 🚫 Use stock photos of children. They feel fake and parents can tell.
- 🚫 Promise specific exam results ("we guarantee an A"). It's both untrue and
  unethical.
- 🚫 Use jargon: "holistic pedagogical frameworks", "scaffolded learning journeys",
  "differentiated instruction". Parents switch off.
- 🚫 Bury the contact details. They're a parent looking for help; don't make them hunt.
- 🚫 Auto-play music or video. Ever.
- 🚫 Pop-ups, modals, chat-bots that appear before the user has done anything. The
  fastest way to lose trust is to interrupt before you've earned attention.
